How Overtone works

Overtone writes a piece of music whose spectrogram, the picture of a sound’s pitches over time, is the scene you describe.

No AI

Everything happens in this page. Overtone reads your sentence for who is in it, what they do and what they do it to, then acts that out with posed figures: eating takes bites until the food is gone, drinking tips the cup, playing strums the instrument. Names and things it doesn’t have a drawing for, like “Leonardo da Vinci” or “the Eiffel Tower”, are looked up on Wikipedia, and the article’s freely licensed pictures are traced into line art and credited in your video. Only those words are sent to Wikipedia, and you can turn this off. It can act out up to two actions, like “a girl running and jumping”. It fixes small typos, traces everyday things it has no drawing for from the open Noto Emoji set, and writes out any word it still doesn’t know.

From picture to sound

  1. Left to right is time, bottom to top is pitch, and brightness is loudness.
  2. Every bright pixel becomes a quiet sine wave at its pitch, tuned to the overtones of the current chord so the picture sounds like part of the music.
  3. A bass line forms the ground, with arpeggios and drums on top. Moving things, like a thrown javelin or a launching rocket, are traced by melody notes.
  4. The screen is a live spectrogram of the audio. Nothing is drawn on it directly: open the WAV in any spectrogram viewer, like Audacity or Spek, and the same picture appears.

What it can draw

Remix and record

Every prompt gets its own key, tempo, chords, bass line, drum feel and melody, picked from its words: fast words like “racing” speed it up, calm words like “sleeping” slow it down. Remix keeps your scene but rolls a new arrangement. Record video types your prompt, paints it and saves an MP4 with sound. The video uses the spectrogram computed from the finished audio, so it scrolls smoothly even if your computer is busy.

Images and videos

A picture becomes a line drawing, or keeps its shading with Tones. A video becomes a film strip of its frames, and Length decides how many. Files never leave your computer.
